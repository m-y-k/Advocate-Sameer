Types of Bail We Handle

  • Regular bail after arrest (Section 483 BNSS / Section 437 CrPC)
  • Anticipatory bail before arrest (Section 482 BNSS / Section 438 CrPC)
  • Default bail when chargesheet is not filed in time (Section 479 BNSS)
  • Interim bail in urgent circumstances
  • Bail before Sessions Court for non-bailable offences
  • Bail before Delhi High Court in serious matters
  • Opposition to bail cancellation applications

Bail Process — Step by Step

Arrest
Person is arrested and produced before Magistrate within 24 hours
Remand
Police applies for custody; accused can apply for bail at this stage
Regular Bail Application
Filed before Magistrate (bailable) or Sessions Court (non-bailable)
Hearing
Both sides argue; court considers nature of offence, antecedents, flight risk
Bail Order
Court grants bail with conditions (surety, attendance, passport surrender etc.)
Release
Accused released upon fulfilling bail conditions

Important Factors Courts Consider for Bail

  • Nature and gravity of the accusation
  • Criminal antecedents of the accused
  • Possibility of the accused fleeing from justice
  • Whether the accused is a first-time offender
  • Risk of the accused tampering with evidence or influencing witnesses
  • Health, age and personal circumstances of the accused

Frequently Asked Questions

Anticipatory bail under Section 482 BNSS (formerly Section 438 CrPC) protects a person from arrest when there is a reasonable apprehension of being arrested for a non-bailable offence. It should be applied for immediately upon receiving notice from police or when there is credible information about an impending arrest. Time is critical — contact a lawyer immediately.

In a bailable offence, bail is a matter of right and the police or Magistrate must grant it. In a non-bailable offence, bail is not automatic — the accused must apply before a Sessions Court or High Court (for serious offences) and the court has discretion to grant or refuse bail based on multiple factors including flight risk, nature of offence, and criminal antecedents.

Default bail (also called statutory bail) arises under Section 479 BNSS when police fails to file a chargesheet within the prescribed period — 60 days for offences punishable with less than 10 years imprisonment, and 90 days for offences punishable with 10 years or more. If the accused applies for bail at this stage, it must be granted.
