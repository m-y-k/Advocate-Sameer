Trial proceedings

Defences Available to the Accused

  • The cheque was not issued against a legally enforceable debt or liability
  • The legal notice was defective or not properly served
  • The complaint was filed after the limitation period
  • The cheque was given as a security deposit and not for discharge of liability
  • The signature on the cheque was forged or the cheque was issued under coercion

Frequently Asked Questions

When a cheque is returned unpaid by a bank — most commonly for insufficient funds — the payee can file a criminal complaint under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881. The drawer can face imprisonment up to 2 years, a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both.

Step 1 — Receive the bank return memo. Step 2 — Send a legal demand notice within 30 days of receiving the memo. Step 3 — If payment is not made within 15 days of the notice, file a complaint before the Judicial Magistrate within 1 month. An advocate handles all these steps on your behalf.

A Section 138 NI Act case in Delhi courts typically takes 1–3 years depending on court workload and complexity. Dwarka District Court has dedicated Magistrate courts for NI Act cases which aid faster disposal.

Yes. Section 138 cases can be compounded (settled) at any stage, even after conviction. If the drawer pays the cheque amount with agreed compensation, the complainant can withdraw the case. Courts actively encourage such settlements.
