What is a Cheque Bounce Case?

A cheque bounce — technically known as dishonour of cheque — occurs when a bank returns a cheque unpaid, most commonly due to insufficient funds in the drawer's account. Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, cheque dishonour is a criminal offence. The drawer can face imprisonment of up to 2 years, a fine of up to twice the cheque amount, or both.

At Sameer Khan & Associates, we represent both complainants (those whose cheques were dishonoured) and accused persons (those facing Section 138 summons) in courts across Delhi, with a strong track record at Dwarka District Court.

Steps to File a Cheque Bounce Case

  1. Receive the Return Memo — When the bank dishonours a cheque, it issues a return memo. Keep this document safely.
  2. Send a Legal Notice — A demand notice must be sent to the drawer within 30 days of receiving the return memo, demanding payment within 15 days.
  3. File the Complaint — If payment is not made within 15 days of the notice, a complaint must be filed in the Magistrate's court within 1 month.
  4. Trial Proceedings — The court issues summons to the accused. The case proceeds through evidence, cross-examination and arguments.
  5. Judgment — The Magistrate delivers a verdict. Both conviction and acquittal can be appealed in the Sessions Court and Delhi High Court.
