How a Criminal Case Proceeds in Delhi

FIR Registration
Police station records the complaint
Investigation
Police investigates, may summon the accused
Chargesheet / Closure Report
Police files chargesheet or closure report in court
Cognizance & Summons
Magistrate takes cognizance and issues summons
Bail Application
Accused applies for bail before Magistrate or Sessions Court
Trial
Evidence, examination of witnesses, cross-examination, arguments
Judgment
Court convicts or acquits the accused
Appeal
Aggrieved party may appeal before Sessions Court or High Court

Your Legal Rights in a Criminal Matter

  • Right to legal representation at every stage of investigation and trial
  • Right to silence — you cannot be forced to incriminate yourself
  • Right to bail — depends on the offence; most offences are bailable
  • Right to be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours of arrest
  • Right to a fair, impartial and timely trial
  • Right to challenge an illegal arrest or detention through Habeas Corpus

An FIR can be quashed by the Delhi High Court under Section 528 BNSS (formerly Section 482 CrPC) if the High Court is satisfied that the FIR discloses no cognisable offence, or continuing prosecution would be an abuse of process of law. An experienced criminal lawyer can assess the merits and file a quashing petition.

Regular bail is applied for after a person has been arrested. Anticipatory bail (under Section 438 CrPC / Section 482 BNSS) is applied for before arrest, when there is a reasonable apprehension of arrest. Anticipatory bail protects the applicant from arrest and allows them to cooperate with investigation while remaining free.
