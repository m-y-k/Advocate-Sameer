Laws Applicable to Divorce in India

  • Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 — for Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists
  • Special Marriage Act, 1954 — for inter-religion marriages and civil marriages
  • Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act — for Muslims
  • Indian Divorce Act, 1869 — for Christians
  • Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936 — for Parsis

Frequently Asked Questions

In mutual consent divorce under Section 13B of the Hindu Marriage Act, both spouses agree to end the marriage. It requires two court appearances (two motions) with a minimum 6-month gap (waivable by court). Contested divorce is filed when one spouse does not consent, citing grounds such as cruelty, desertion, adultery or mental illness. Contested divorce typically takes longer.

A mutual consent divorce in Delhi can be completed in approximately 6–18 months. After the first motion, there is a mandatory 6-month waiting period (though courts can waive this). The second motion must be filed within 18 months. If both parties cooperate and have a clear settlement agreement, the process can be faster.

Grounds for divorce under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 include: cruelty, desertion for two or more years, conversion to another religion, unsoundness of mind, leprosy (in severe form), venereal disease, renunciation of the world, and presumption of death. Adultery is also a ground. Women have an additional ground of husband's bigamy.

Additionally, she can file a separate maintenance application under Section 125 CrPC (now BNSS) before a Magistrate, which does not require a pending divorce case.
